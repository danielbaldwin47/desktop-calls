# The Dialer: search, a centred layer-shell overlay, and its latency

Date: 2026-11-05. Ticket #11. Stack as in the [screen harness note](2026-11-04-screen-harness.md): GTK 4 with gtk4-layer-shell on sway 1.12 headless, the Simulated Phone over PBAP. No real Phone was used.
Legend: **[V]** = verified on this machine. **[I]** = inferred, not tested.

**Bottom line:** the Dialer is shown 12 ms after `Activate` with 5000 Contacts, and its rows follow each keystroke within 8 ms, in a debug build. The limits are 150 ms and 50 ms. Matching runs on the main thread inside the Call state machine's step, and it is fast enough there. No worker thread or release profile was needed.

## 1. Latency with 5000 Contacts

`with_5000_contacts_the_dialer_shows_and_follows_each_keystroke_promptly` (`crates/test-harness/tests/dialer_surface.rs`) times two things: `Activate` until `Surfaces` reports the Dialer mapped, and each synthetic keystroke of `contact 49` until `Surfaces` reports the new query. That report comes after the step has searched and the GTK list has been rebuilt. **[V]**

| What | Debug build, headless sway |
|---|---|
| `Activate` until the Dialer is shown | 12 ms |
| Slowest keystroke until its rows are shown | 8 ms |

- Each list holds at most 50 rows (`matching::MAX_ROWS`), so rebuilding the GTK list stays cheap. **[V]**
- Pre-warming the window with `realize()`, as the Banner already does, keeps the first show fast. **[I]**: this is the Banner's measured reason; the Dialer's first show was not timed without it.
- The 12 ms and 8 ms come from one machine. A slow CI runner will be slower, but the margin is more than 6 times. **[I]**

## 2. Centring and focus on the layer shell

- A layer-shell surface anchored to no edge is centred by sway in the area other surfaces' exclusive zones leave, which here is below the bar. The scenario checks the screenshot's centre against `(LAYOUT_SIZE.1 + BAR_HEIGHT) / 2` to within 2 px. **[V]** Hyprland is expected to centre the same way. **[I]**
- `KeyboardMode::Exclusive` on the overlay layer gives the Dialer the keyboard as soon as it is mapped. `focused` in `Surfaces` comes from GTK's `is-active`. **[V]**
- `GtkEntry::grab_focus()` selects the whole text. When the Dialer opened with a query (`desktop-calls dialer jose`), that made the first keystroke replace the query. The gallery screenshot showed this. `grab_focus_without_selecting()` and the cursor at the end fix it, and a scenario now types after an opening query. **[V]**

## 3. Matching, and Foner as the oracle

- Foner's search cases (`AminBlg/kaller@fc25b13`, `tests/test_contacts.cpp`) are in `crates/core/tests/matching.rs`, with numbers from Ofcom's drama ranges. **[V]**
- Two cases do not carry over:
  - T9: Foner's keypad queries are typed as the letters they spell, because the Dialer is typed on a keyboard and digit queries match numbers.
  - "An empty query keeps everything": the spec lists the Call History before typing instead.
- Accents fold through Unicode canonical decomposition (`unicode-normalization`), plus a short table for letters that don't decompose (ß, æ, ø, ł and others). **[V]**

## 4. Choices made, and their limits

- **The Dialer's state lives in the core.** The Call state machine holds the query, rows, selection and notice, and recomputes the rows when Contacts or the Call History change. The GTK entry is the one copy of what is typed. The core puts text into it only when the Dialer opens (`DialerView::opened`), so fast typing never races a redraw. **[V]**
- **Enter that can't place the Call** sets the Dialer's notice and leaves the Dialer open, with no "Call not placed" Call Window. This applies to a disconnected Phone, a Call in progress, a withheld number and an undialable number. **[V]**
- **`OpenDialer(query, select_call_id)`** selects the Call History row of the same number as the Call. The core remembers the other party of the last 16 Calls. When the Phone hasn't refreshed the Call History yet and no row shows that number, the first row is selected. #12 is expected to refine this. **[I]**
- **Call History times** are shown in the Desktop's time zone at its offset now, from GLib (`messages::when`). Across a daylight-saving change, a time from before it is off by an hour. **[I]**
