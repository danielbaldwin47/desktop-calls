# Testing

Dated facts about how the app is tested: the harness, CI against the Desktop, and the Real-Phone Checklist. Page added 2026-11-05 (batch 3). Earlier testing history lives in [session-log](session-log.md) and [polish-scorecard](polish-scorecard.md); the test seam itself is in [product-shape](product-shape.md) (Q27).

## Timings: CI against the Desktop

- 2026-11-05 (#47, `3def480`): CI's runner paints in software. (As of `700e1aa`, "CI" for a PR means `just ci-local` on the Desktop, which paints in software too; GitHub's runner runs the same jobs on pushes to `main`. See "CI runs locally" below.) Run alone there, the Dialer's first frame, warm, took 150.4 ms (6 ms to map, 144 ms for its own first paint), against 97 to 102 ms under `just test-ci-like`. So CI holds it to 250 ms, a regression bound that still catches shaders compiling cold (306 ms and more). Locally and under `just test-ci-like` (which sets `DESKTOP_CALLS_CI_LIKE`) the bound is 150 ms. The 150 ms Desktop target is judged on the real Desktop, at Real-Phone Checklist step 7.4, from the app-logged time between `activate-requested` and the Dialer's `surface-shown`.
- 2026-11-05 (#47, `3def480`): the two first-frame timing tests (`the_first_banner_shows_without_holding_up_the_main_loop` and `with_5000_contacts_the_dialer_shows_and_follows_each_keystroke_promptly`) are `#[ignore]`d in the whole run. CI runs them in a step of their own, one scenario at a time (`-- --ignored --test-threads=1`). With four scenarios at once, every scenario's app compiles shaders together, which no Desktop does. The Dialer, first-Banner and idle scenarios wait for `first-frames-prepared` (`wait_for_first_frames_prepared`) before they act. Source: [first frames](../research/2026-11-05-first-frames.md).
- 2026-11-05 (#17, `417411e`): the Do Not Disturb toggle scenario holds 250 ms from the harness replacing `quiet.json` to the ringer's log line. The app's own part was under 1 ms; the rest is inotify and the main loop. Source: [Do Not Disturb silent](../research/2026-11-05-do-not-disturb-silent.md).

## CI runs locally

- 2026-11-05 (#158): `just ci-local` runs CI's jobs on this machine, in `archlinux:latest` with CI's packages, unprivileged (`unshare` and `chroot`, no sudo or Docker), and posts the commit status "CI passed" on HEAD when they pass. GitHub runs full CI only on pushes to main; `just merge` reads the status as it read the check. The image, cargo cache and logs are in `~/.cache/desktop-calls-ci` (never `/tmp`, which has a per-user quota); a lock makes agents' runs take turns. Keep `just test-ci-like` for timing on 2 CPUs.
- 2026-11-05 (#158, PR #159, `700e1aa`), how it is used: agents run `just ci-local` before every push (the [implementer brief](../agents/implementer-brief.md)), and a new commit, including a rebase onto a newer `main`, needs a fresh run, because the status is on one exact commit. Seen on #143 (`955394b`): a rebase onto #153 (`97278a3`) broke a Setup Window keyboard test, and the fresh local run caught it before the push.
- 2026-11-05 (#154 comments, from PRs #143, #157 and #159; part superseded on 2026-11-06: `a_killed_just_run_still_starts_the_installed_service_again` was fixed by `7c8b607` (#155, PR #156), as #154's triage comment of 2026-11-05 records, so it is not a live flake; `handoff_recovery::with_connect_profile_failing_once_the_call_is_picked_back_up_when_hands_free_returns` was #170, fixed in the app by PR #174, `95f225f`; the timing flakes are under "Local CI under load" below): local runs share the Desktop with agents' builds, so timing and recovery tests are load-sensitive. Seen in the first hours: `handoff_recovery::with_connect_profile_failing_once_the_call_is_picked_back_up_when_hands_free_returns` (PR #143), a Dialer first frame at 172 ms against 50 ms under load 15 (PR #159), `the_first_banner_shows_without_holding_up_the_main_loop` (main loop held 104 ms, PR #157's CI run), and `a_killed_just_run_still_starts_the_installed_service_again` (PR #142's CI run). The list is in the comments of #154, which is the source of truth; if flakes keep landing, #154 proposes fewer scenario threads or waiting for low load before the timing jobs.

## Local CI under load (2026-11-05 evening into 2026-11-06)

- 2026-11-05 (#167, PR #173, `4c8dc97`): the cause of the first load failures was that `just ci-local` ran every test runner at one test per CPU (20 on the Desktop, against 2 on CI's runners) under `nice 19`, beside agents' `cargo build`s at load 10 to 20. Not the shared `/tmp`: the image already gives each run a private one. Now it watches what other programs use for 3 s and sets threads to `clamp(cpus - 2 * others, 4, cpus)` (`RUST_TEST_THREADS`, `NEXTEST_TEST_THREADS`, and `--workers` for the gallery); the timing tests run as their own job, which waits up to 120 s for 3 quiet 2 s looks in a row and then runs without `nice`; and each job's last line says "other programs used about N of 20 CPUs while it ran" and, on failure, whether that is enough to blame. No bound was loosened. A quiet Desktop is unchanged (179 s); a busy one takes 5 to 6 minutes a run instead of about 3.
- 2026-11-05 (PR #173's evidence, run by its agent): with a build running, 3 of 5 back-to-back runs were green after the change; the two red runs failed on #170, #171 and #172, which were then fixed (below). The change is **partial**: #167's "Done when" (5 of 5 green beside a build) was not met, and `gh issue view 167` showed it still open on 2026-11-06. A 5 ms judgement beside a build that restarts every few seconds can't be made reliable by waiting (the first-frame timing read 254.7 ms against the 250 ms bound once).
- 2026-11-05 (PR #173): checklist step 6.1's 200 ms bound can still read about 190 ms on a busy Desktop, because its timing starts at the app's `phone-present` log, which is later than BlueZ's mark. Left as is; noted on #154.
- 2026-11-06 (reading #173 and #154's comments together): rerunning until green hides real flakes and costs about 3 minutes a run, so each flake below got its own ticket and was fixed at its cause, by event order and not by a looser bound.

### Load flakes, by cause

Each was first filed as a flake under load; the PR that fixed it found where the cause was. PR #173's own table guessed some of these causes, and the later diagnoses supersede it: it called #168 "app or harness", #169 "app" and #171 "race".

- In the app: **#170** (`with_connect_profile_failing_once…`). Hands-free came back 12 ms before the 2 s grace for a vanished Call ran out and the Call was listed 28 ms after, so a live Call showed "Call ended" and came back as Call 2. Fixed by PR #174 (`95f225f`), and recorded as [ADR-0007](../adr/0007-a-vanished-call-is-settled-by-the-phones-calls-once-hands-free-is-back.md): once hands-free is back, a vanished Call is settled by the Calls the Phone lists, within a 3 s `LISTING_GRACE`.
- In the Simulated Phone: **#169** (a Hang up while the service is away). The app had kept the Hang up and sent it to the Call that came back; the Simulated Phone then announced the Call again from a list made before the restart, and the app took it as Call 2. The issue's log excerpt had left out `call-returned`, which is why it looked like an app race. Fixed by PR #174: after a restart the Calls leave the service with it and are registered only when the new service announces them. **#171** (`a_phone_that_forgets_the_pairing…`). A `Connect` already under way when the pairing was forgotten finished as a success, where the real Phone fails it with `br-connection-key-missing`. Fixed by PR #177 (`7af1a67`); the app already handled that failure.
- In the test: **#172** (`after_resume_the_phone_is_connected_within_10_s`) used a fixed 200 ms sleep as a guess that the app had handled the suspend, and counted the Connect the app sent at startup. It now waits for the app's `desktop-suspending` log line and compares against the count before. PR #178 (`198f4e9`) could not reproduce the sleep half by event order; the wait removes the guess. **#185** (`dev_runs::a_dev_run_stopped_mid_call_leaves_nothing_for_the_installed_service`) read the log before the app had logged `earlier-run-loaded`, because the app claims its bus name first on purpose. PR #186 (`f5b3e4c`) reproduced it by event order, holding the record behind a named pipe.
- In the harness: **#168** (`the_installed_service_picks_up_its_own_calls_after_a_crash`). The scenario's private session bus ran the service file's `Exec=` itself, so an app activated by the bus had no `RUNTIME_DIRECTORY` and kept its record in `desktop-calls-dev`. The bus now activates through the systemd stand-in (`dbus-daemon --systemd-activation`). Fixed by PR #179 (`a7a6809`); which in-test caller reached for the app's name in the failing run was never identified. **#180** (`clicking_answer_answers_and_the_call_window_shows_the_audio_here_and_a_timer`). The harness read the app's log mid-write and panicked on an unterminated last line. `crates/test-harness/src/app_log.rs` now returns only newline-terminated lines. Fixed by PR #184 (`26e232f`); the cause was inferred from the code and the tests reproduce the partial line, but it was never reproduced against the real app. #180 failed at low load ("other programs used about 1 of 20 CPUs", per the issue), so PR #173's load handling would not have prevented it.

## Stand-ins must be as strict as the real service

- 2026-11-05 (PR #166, #162, `9ebed55`): the review found the systemd stand-in in the Simulated Phone was more lenient than systemd. It sent `JobRemoved` to every client, so the app could drop its `Subscribe` call and every test still passed. The stand-in now sends `JobRemoved` only to clients that called `Subscribe` (as `org.freedesktop.systemd1(5)` says), fails `Unsubscribe` without `Subscribe`, numbers jobs from 7, and sends an unrelated job's end first, before the `RestartUnit` reply. With the app's `Subscribe` removed, 6 of 20 `setup_checks` scenarios then fail; with the job-path match removed, 11 of 20. That the real user manager behaves so is unverified: the stand-in follows the documented interface.
- 2026-11-05 (same PR): the permission tests used mode 0640, which a umask of 027 produces unaided, so they could pass without the fix. They use 0604.
- Rule (the chief of staff's lesson from #166, 2026-11-05), seen again in PR #176 (the Simulated Phone's `restart_telephony` now closes the old service's connection, so dbus-daemon answers an unread call with `NoReply`; inferred, not seen on a real WirePlumber restart) and PR #177 (a `Connect` fails after the pairing is forgotten): a stand-in is held to the real service's rules for everything the app depends on. When a test passes only because the stand-in is kind, fix the stand-in, and prove it by removing the call the app depends on and watching scenarios fail.

## Harness

- 2026-11-05 (#107, `7032137`): the harness records `Calls` and `Surfaces` after each `PropertiesChanged`, in the order the app sent them (`Harness::published`), and `surfaces_seen` derives from it. `crates/test-harness/tests/publish_order.rs` checks that a surface names a Call only once `Calls` lists it, and closes only once `Calls` says why. In the app, `Surfaces::render` and `Indicator::render` take a `Published` proof that only `Publisher::publish` makes, so rendering before publishing doesn't compile. Source: [publish order](../research/2026-11-05-publish-order.md).
- 2026-11-05 (#19, `6bf11cb`): the Simulated Phone's `Disconnect` means out of range, and it gained `ReturnToRange`, `ForgetPairing`, `CallsOnPhone` and `PrepareForSleep(b)` (it owns `org.freedesktop.login1` on the private system bus). `Battery1` exists only while hands-free is up, as on the Pixel. Source: [out of range](../research/2026-11-05-out-of-range.md).
- 2026-11-05: new gallery states: `call-window-connection-lost` (#101, `f7a35a6`) and `incoming-call-banner-identifying` (#103, `f6ecaae`). The latter is captured inside a 2 s window and can silently show "Withheld number" on a slow runner (#115).

## Real-Phone Checklist

- 2026-11-05 (#120, PR #132): results from the real Phone live in `~/.local/state/desktop-calls/real-phone-checklist/` (`$XDG_STATE_HOME` if it is absolute), never in the repository. Cite that path in summaries. Without an interactive terminal the runner says so in one line and writes nothing. "Before you start" lists only the needs of the steps about to run.
- 2026-11-05 (#17, `417411e`; extended by #107, `7032137`): step 2.7 is scripted. Under Do Not Disturb, the user declines one Call on the Desktop and answers a second on the Pixel; the runner fails the step if the second looks handled on the Desktop, and `Ringing` must stay false throughout. Under Do Not Disturb the runner reads "handled on the Desktop" from the Banner closing while the Call still rings. The user's next sitting is the first real run.
- 2026-11-05 (#19, `6bf11cb`): six out-of-range checks await the user (walk out and back, suspend, a Call to the Test Number out of range, the real `Connect` errors, A2DP and paging cost, the link supervision timeout). Agents must not run them. They are listed in [out of range](../research/2026-11-05-out-of-range.md); the paging cost also gates #109.
- 2026-11-05 (#101, `f7a35a6`): no item added, because nothing safe makes `ConnectProfile` fail on the real Pixel.
