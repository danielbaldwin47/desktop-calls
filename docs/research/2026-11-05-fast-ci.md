# Where CI's time went, and the four changes that bring it under 4 minutes

Date: 2026-11-05. Ticket #118, PR #131 (which absorbed #136, #137 and #138).
Legend: **[V]** = verified (where is stated). **[I]** = inferred, not tested.

**Bottom line:** on the public 4-vCPU runners, main's CI took 461 s. With all four changes together it took 160 s with a warm cache, and 245 s with a cold one (draft PR #141, measurement only, now closed). Billed minutes go from 16 to 17 a run warm, 24 cold, which is free on a public repo. **[V]**

## Where the time went

Measured on the old private repo's 2-vCPU runners (profiling run 42034428129) and on the public 4-vCPU runners (main run 42044794049). **[V]**

- Two jobs ran side by side. The critical path was the check job: `just check` (178 s on 4 vCPU) then every test but the harness's (213 s). `just check` built every target, the harness's test binaries among them, and nothing in that job ran those tests.
- The gallery in that job rendered 64 shots one at a time, each with its own compositor, buses and app: 205 s on 2 vCPU.
- The harness job ran 31 test binaries one after another under `cargo test`. Each binary's slowest scenario held the other CPUs idle at its end: on 2 vCPU, 326 s with the CPUs 66% busy. Under cargo-nextest, which pools every binary's scenarios, the same 209 scenarios took 231 s at the same 2 threads.
- The 209 scenarios sum to 457 s of scenario time. The slowest: `contacts::an_unanswered_prompt_needs_permission_until_the_phone_gives_up` 30 s (#139), `out_of_range` 14 s, `handoff_recovery` 12 s, `contacts` with 5000 contacts 12 s. By file: contacts 63 s, handoff 52 s, call_window_keyboard 41 s.
- Linking the harness's 31 test binaries cost about a quarter of a workspace rebuild: 24.0 s for every target on `-j 2` locally, 17.5 s as one binary.
- More scenarios at once than CPUs breaks timing scenarios: on 2 vCPU, 4 threads failed 3 Banner and Handoff timings, and 6 hung `call_window_keyboard`.

## The four changes, each measured alone

Each PR's own CI run on the public runners, warm cache, against main's 461 s. **[V]**

| PR | Change | Wall clock | Billed minutes |
|---|---|---|---|
| main | before | 461 s | 16 |
| #131 | `just lint` in its own job; no third build of every target | 276 s | 12 |
| #136 | the gallery renders one shot per CPU at a time | 275 s | 11 |
| #137 | the harness's scenarios in 4 nextest shards | 259 s | 18 |
| #138 | the harness's test files as one binary; `just test-file` | 322 s | 12 |
| #141 | all four together, warm | 160 s | 17 |
| #141 | all four together, cold cache | 245 s | 24 |

## Options not taken

| Option | Wall clock | Minutes | Why not |
|---|---|---|---|
| Larger hosted runners (8+ vCPU) | about the same as 4 shards **[I]** | billed even on a public repo | costs money |
| Self-hosted runners | fastest **[I]** | 0 | a security risk on a public repo |
| 8 shards instead of 4 | shards are no longer the critical path **[I]** | +8 a run | no gain until the other test job is faster |
| sccache or a shared target cache | saves the 20 s per-shard build **[I]** | 0 | rust-cache already caches dependencies; more cache costs storage and restore time |
| Shorter give-ups in slow scenarios | the 30 s contacts scenario is each shard's floor | 0 | app and harness code: #139 |

## What's left on the critical path

- The job running every test but the harness's is now the longest. **[V]** in #141. Its gallery and the automation crate's tests could be split across two jobs the way the harness's are. **[I]**
- Each job spends about 20 s installing Arch packages and about 10 s starting the container. **[V]** A prebuilt image would cut that, at the cost of maintaining the image. **[I]**
