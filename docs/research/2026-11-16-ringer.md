# Ringer: where the ring comes from, and a concept for a Ringer Window

Date: 2026-11-16. From the owner's note after regular use: the ring on an incoming Call is "super noisy and jarring"; they want a mellow default "more like macOS", a dedicated Ringer page, and everything configurable (which sound, volume, a different sound for particular Contacts). Read-only research by one agent: nothing on the Phone, BlueZ, WirePlumber or the installed service was changed.
Legend: **[V]** = verified, from this repo's code at `fd7469a`, the installed binaries, or the user journal on the Desktop (source named). **[I]** = inferred.

**Bottom line:** the ring the owner hears from the Desktop is almost certainly the app's own: it loops the freedesktop sound theme's `phone-incoming-call.oga` with `pw-play` at full stream volume on the default output, back to back with no pause. That file measures **-6.8 LUFS** integrated, about 24 LU louder than the same theme's `message-new-instant` (-30.4 LUFS), at a near-constant -3 dBFS peak: loud by any measure, and "jarring" fits it. The Phone's in-band ringtone cannot reach the Desktop while nothing is live: the Pixel does advertise and enable in-band ring, but the app keeps `RejectSCO` true whenever no Call is live, and PipeWire then refuses the Phone's audio link. The Pixel itself still rings in the room unless silenced. One test at the next Call settles it: turn Do Not Disturb on (the app's sound is off, the Banner still shows) and listen.

## 1. Where the ring comes from today

### The app's own sound

- **What plays.** `crates/desktop-calls/src/ringer.rs` loops `phone-incoming-call` from the freedesktop sound theme (`sounds/freedesktop/stereo/`, first of `.oga`, `.ogg`, `.wav`), looked up in `$XDG_DATA_HOME` (only if set) then `$XDG_DATA_DIRS`. **[V]** (code) On this Desktop that is `/usr/share/sounds/freedesktop/stereo/phone-incoming-call.oga` from `sound-theme-freedesktop` 0.8. **[V]** (`pacman -Qo`)
- **How it is chosen.** Hard-coded: the constant `SOUND`. There is no setting. **[V]** (code)
- **How it plays.** `pw-play <file>`, one play at a time, the next started as each ends, so the 1.46 s clip repeats with no gap until stopped. **[V]** (code; duration by `ffprobe`)
- **At what volume.** `pw-play`'s default stream volume 1.0, no `--volume`, default media role `Music`. **[V]** (code; `pw-play --help`) WirePlumber restores the stream volume under the key `application.name:pw-play`, stored here as 1.0 and shared with anything else that runs `pw-play`. **[V]** (`~/.local/state/wireplumber/stream-properties`) The only level control the user has today is the output's own volume. **[I]**
- **Loudness.** `ffmpeg` `ebur128`/`volumedetect` over the theme's files **[V]**:

  | File | Length | Integrated | Peak |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | `phone-incoming-call` (today's ring) | 1.46 s | -6.8 LUFS | -2.8 dBFS, held throughout |
  | `alarm-clock-elapsed` | 6.13 s | -9.3 LUFS | -5.7 dBFS |
  | `complete` | 1.09 s | -17.1 LUFS | -3.1 dBFS, then decays |
  | `message-new-instant` | 1.03 s | -30.4 LUFS | -15.4 dBFS, then decays |

- **Through which output.** The default sink, whatever it is when each play starts; nothing chooses a device. **[V]** (code) On this Desktop today that is the audio interface's "Headphone + Monitor Out" at volume 1.00. **[V]** (`wpctl status`)
- **When it rings.** `State::ringing()` (`crates/core/src/machine.rs`): a Call rings here (Ringing or Waiting, no request sent), no other Call is live or blocks ringing, Do Not Disturb is off, and the Calls are within reach; `step` turns its edges into `StartRinging`/`StopRinging`. **[V]** (code) So a Waiting Call never rings over a live one, and under Do Not Disturb the Banner shows silently. Under Calls Off, including Quiet Hours, the Phone is disconnected, so no Call reaches the Desktop and nothing rings. **[V]** (ADR-0008, code)
- **At the owner's Call of 2026-11-16 20:03.** The journal shows `Call 1 appeared, ringing` at 20:03:19 and `Call 1 ended` at 20:03:23, with no `Couldn't play the ringtone` warning. **[V]** (journal) The ringer's own started and stopped lines are debug level and aren't in the journal, so that it played is **[I]**, from the missing warning. The same window has a GLib warning `waitid(pid:...) failed: No child processes` at 20:03:22, just as ringing stopped; that it is the ringer reaping `pw-play` twice is **[I]**.

### The Phone's in-band ringtone

- **The Pixel offers in-band ring and turns it on.** In WirePlumber's debug log of the first connection (2026-11-04), the Pixel answered `AT+BRSF=695` with `+BRSF: 3951`, which has bit 3, "in-band ring tone capability", set; then it sent `+BSIR: 0` and, 37 ms later, `+BSIR: 1`, leaving in-band ring on. **[V]** (journal, `spa.bluez5.native`)
- **The Desktop has no say in that bit.** In HFP the in-band bit is the audio gateway's (the Phone's); the hands-free unit's `AT+BRSF` has no bit to refuse it, and only the gateway sends `+BSIR`. **[I]** from the HFP feature tables; consistent with PipeWire's 695 having no such bit. PipeWire 1.6.9's bluez5 plugin contains no `BSIR` handling string at all. **[V]** (`strings libspa-bluez5.so`) So "not advertising in-band ring support" is not an option on this side.
- **But the Desktop refuses the link that would carry it.** In-band ring means the Phone opens the audio link (SCO) and plays its ringtone down it. PipeWire refuses any incoming SCO while `RejectSCO` is true, logging `rejecting SCO, AudioGatewayTransport1.RejectSCO=true`; that line is in the binary and was seen six times on 2026-11-04 when the Pixel tried to open audio during the Handoff prototype. **[V]** (binary, journal)
- **`RejectSCO` is true whenever no Call is live.** It defaults true (`bluez5.telephony.default-reject-sco = true` in `60-desktop-calls.conf`, checked by `crates/desktop-calls/src/wireplumber.rs`), is set true again when hands-free connects (`machine/phone.rs`), when the last Call ends (`machine/calls.rs`), and once audio the Desktop asked for has arrived (`machine/audio.rs`, ADR-0005). It is false only between the Desktop's Answer or Handoff and the audio arriving, and after a Handoff the Phone ignored ("Audio not moved") until that Call ends. **[V]** (code)
- **So, for a Call that rings while nothing else is live, the Pixel's ringtone never reaches the Desktop.** **[I]**, from the above; at the 20:03 Call WirePlumber logged at its default level, which omits the `rejecting SCO` info line, so whether the Pixel even tried is unseen. What the Pixel does when the link is refused (ring on its own speaker, retry) is **[I]**; the owner hears the Pixel's own ringer in the room either way unless the Pixel is silenced.
- **Where the Phone's audio can reach the Desktop:** a Waiting Call while a Call's audio is already on the Desktop. The link is open, so the Pixel's call-waiting tone plays in the Call audio, as on any headset. **[I]** (standard HFP behaviour; not observed) The app's ringer stays silent then by design.

### What settles it at the next Call

1. **Do Not Disturb on, then a Call** (Real-Phone Checklist step 2.7 does exactly this). The Banner shows and the app plays nothing. If the Desktop is silent apart from the Pixel itself, the jarring sound was the app's. If a ringtone still comes out of the Desktop's output, it is the Phone's in-band ring.
2. Optionally, while it rings: `pw-dump | grep -E '"application.name": "pw-play"|bluez_(input|output)'` shows a `pw-play` stream (the app's ring) and whether any Bluetooth audio node exists (the Phone's audio on the Desktop); `wpctl status` shows the same under Streams.

## 2. Silencing or replacing the in-band ring

Today it is already silenced on the Desktop, as a side effect of keeping Audio Location on the Phone until answered. The options, should that ever change or should a "use the Phone's ringtone" setting be wanted:

| Option | What it does | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Keep `RejectSCO` true until answer (today) | Refuses the Phone's link during ringing | None now. Rules out "use the Phone's ringtone" |
| Stop advertising in-band support | Not possible: the bit is the Phone's, not the hands-free unit's **[I]** | n/a |
| Turn off in-band ring on the Pixel | Android's developer option "Disable in-band ringing" **[I]** | A Phone setting, outside the app (ADR-0001 needs nothing on the Phone); affects car kits too |
| Accept the link while ringing but mute its node until answer | Lets the Phone's audio connect early, the Desktop discards it | The Phone then believes audio is on the Desktop, so a Call answered on the Phone may keep its audio here, breaking "Audio Location follows the answering device" (product-shape, ADR-0005); WirePlumber and the Call's link change state mid-ring, which the Simulated Phone doesn't model. Not recommended |
| Accept the link while ringing and play it ("use the Phone's ringtone") | The Pixel's own ringtone, per Contact as set on the Phone | Same Audio Location breakage as above, plus narrowband ring quality and a link opened for every Call. Not recommended for the first cut |

## 3. What settings and per-Contact storage exist today

- **Settings surfaces.** One: the Quiet Hours Window (a switch and two times, saved as they change), opened from the Indicator's menu, Alt+Q in the Dialer, or `desktop-calls quiet-hours`. The Setup Window holds pairing, Contacts access and the keybinding. There is no general settings window, and the glossary avoids "settings window" and "preferences" (Quiet Hours Window, `_Avoid_`). **[V]** (GLOSSARY, `crates/desktop-calls/src/surfaces/`)
- **Configuration on disk.** `$XDG_CONFIG_HOME/desktop-calls/quiet-hours`, one hand-editable line such as `22:00-07:00` (`quiet_hours.rs`). State, not configuration, in `$XDG_STATE_HOME/desktop-calls/`: `chosen-phone.json`, `calls-off.json`, `missed-calls`. **[V]** (code) No config holds anything about sound. **[V]**
- **Contacts.** Pulled from the Phone over PBAP and held as `Contact { name, numbers: [{ number, label }] }` (`crates/core/src/contacts.rs`), with no stable identifier: no vCard UID is kept. **[V]** (code) The on-disk copy is one JSON file per Phone, keyed by its Bluetooth address, in `$XDG_CACHE_HOME/desktop-calls/` (0700 directory, 0600 files), rewritten whole on each sync (`contacts/cache.rs`). **[V]** It is a cache of the Phone's data: the app stores **no per-Contact data of its own** today. **[V]**
- **A stopgap that works now.** Because `XDG_DATA_HOME` is set in the user manager's environment (`systemctl --user show-environment`), a file at `~/.local/share/sounds/freedesktop/stereo/phone-incoming-call.oga` replaces the ring at the next Call without a rebuild. **[V]** the variable is set and the lookup reads it; that the service inherits it is **[I]** (systemd user services get the manager's environment). Note that the lookup does not fall back to `~/.local/share` when `XDG_DATA_HOME` is unset, as the XDG spec says it should. **[V]** (code)

## 4. Concept draft

### Proposed terms

- **Ringer**: the sound the Desktop plays while a Call rings here. _Avoid_: ringtone (the Phone's), alert.
- **Ring Sound**: one sound the Ringer can play, picked by name.
- **Contact Ring Sound**: a Ring Sound chosen for one Contact, played instead of the default when that Contact calls.
- **In-Band Ring**: the Phone's own ringtone sent down the hands-free audio link while a Call rings. The Desktop refuses it (section 1).
- **Ringer Window**: the small window where the user chooses the Ring Sound, its volume and Contact Ring Sounds. Opened like the Quiet Hours Window: from the Indicator's menu ("Ringer…"), a Dialer shortcut, and `desktop-calls ringer`. _Avoid_: settings window, preferences.

### Ringer Window contents

1. **Ring Sound** picker: a short list (the mellow default first, then two or three alternates, then "Theme's phone ring" for today's sound, then "Choose a file…"). Each row has a **Play** button that plays it once at the chosen volume, through the same output a real Call would use; moving off the row, or closing the window, stops it.
2. **Volume** slider, 0 to 100 %, previewing as it moves. Applied as the ring stream's own volume, so it sits under the system volume rather than replacing it. Default about 60 %.
3. **Contact Ring Sounds**: a list of Contacts with their own Ring Sound, an **Add** row that searches Contacts as the Dialer does, and Remove per row. A Call from a number not matched to a Contact uses the default.
4. Possibly later: **Silent** as a Ring Sound (Banner only), and per-Contact **Silent**. "Use the Phone's ringtone" is left out (section 2).

Behaviour beside the window, all simple to reverse:

- Each repeat followed by a pause (about 2 s), and a short fade-in over the first repeat, as macOS and phones do.
- The ring stream named "Desktop Calls ringer" with media role `Notification`, so WirePlumber remembers its volume apart from other `pw-play` users and the system's own rules can treat it as an alert.
- Stored in `$XDG_CONFIG_HOME/desktop-calls/ringer`, hand-editable like `quiet-hours`. Contact Ring Sounds keyed by the Contact's numbers, as the app matches callers by number; a Contact renamed on the Phone keeps its sound, a changed number loses it.

### How it fits what exists

- **Do Not Disturb**: unchanged. The Ringer is silent and the Banner shows, Contact Ring Sounds included. A per-Contact "ring even under Do Not Disturb" is a separate question for the owner.
- **Calls Off and Quiet Hours**: unchanged. The Phone is disconnected, so nothing rings; the Ringer Window doesn't need to mention them.
- **Waiting Call**: unchanged, no Ringer over a live Call (spec #1, accepted 2026-11-04).
- **Missed Call cue and notifications**: unchanged. The app plays no sound for them and sends no sound hint; whatever Omarchy's notification daemon does is its own. **[V]** (`notifications.rs`)

### Where a mellow default could come from

All compatible with this repo's GPL-3.0-or-later. Nothing was downloaded; licences marked **[I]** need checking before use.

1. **Synthesised in the app** (recommended): a soft two- or three-note chime (sine partials with a quick attack and a 1 s decay, like a marimba or glass), rendered once to a WAV in the cache at startup or written by a build step. The project owns it, so no licence question and nothing new to install; it can be tuned to about -20 LUFS.
2. **The freedesktop theme already installed**: `message-new-instant.oga` or `complete.oga`, repeated with a pause. Licences in the package are GPL-2.0-or-later, LGPL-2.0-or-later, CC-BY-SA-3.0, CC-BY-3.0 and CC-BY-4.0 by file **[V]** (`pacman -Qi`); used from the system, not copied into the repo, so the per-file licence matters only for redistribution. Not written as ringtones, so they may read as a chat message.
3. **AOSP's ringtones** (`frameworks/base/data/sounds/ringtones/` in Android's open source tree), Apache-2.0, which GPL-3.0 accepts **[I]**. Written as phone rings, several of them gentle. Would be copied into the repo with a notice.
4. **KDE's Ocean sound theme** (CC-BY-SA-4.0, compatible one way with GPL-3.0 **[I]**) or Ubuntu's Yaru sounds, if either ships a `phone-incoming-call` **[I]**; neither is installed here.

### Decisions

For the owner (stores personal data, or hard to undo):

1. Contact Ring Sounds store which Contacts the user singled out, on disk beside the Contacts cache. Build them at all?
2. Key them by number (survives a rename on the Phone, lost on a number change) or by name?
3. Bundle a sound in the public repo (AOSP's, with its licence notice), or synthesise one in code, or rely on a system sound package?
4. Ever offer "use the Phone's ringtone"? It means accepting the Phone's audio before answer, against "Audio Location follows the answering device".
5. Should a Contact be able to ring through Do Not Disturb?

Simple to reverse, so the chief of staff can decide them under the owner's leave of 2026-11-15 and mark them in [decided-for-the-owner](../wiki/decided-for-the-owner.md):

- The new mellow default replaces `phone-incoming-call` for everyone, with today's sound kept as a choice.
- A 2 s pause between repeats, a short fade-in, default volume about 60 %, media role `Notification`, a named stream.
- One Ringer Window, opened like the Quiet Hours Window; config in `$XDG_CONFIG_HOME/desktop-calls/ringer`.
- `sound_file()` falls back to `~/.local/share` when `XDG_DATA_HOME` is unset.
